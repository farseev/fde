What was asked

Every question put, by cohort

Choose three. No recorded session got through more than about three at any one table, so the rest are a reserve rather than a plan.

Cohort 1 · 6 questions
  1. q0We start from elevator pitch… we just introduce ourselves a little bit… [and] a fun fact about yourself.
  2. q1You're going to be work[ing] from acceptance and not towards acceptance… so what typically motivates you to work hard and create more? In what ways can accept[ing] [your] identity in Christ change your motivation to serve others?
  3. q2How does it look like for your motivation to [work] to be loving others, and what it takes to love others — maybe clients, employees, investors, those three categories?
  4. q3Shall we do storytelling and position ourself in a more creative light… or be completely who we are and have a super strong [faith]… or maybe a balance of these two?
  5. q4What [are the] practical steps you could make to improve your product or service that further reflects your heart for your customers?
  6. q5There's [an] exploit[at]ive way of using people… there is [an] ethical way… how do we do it redemptive? How do we sacrifice ourself for our employees…? How did you take one more step further to make it a bit more redemptive in nature — what would you leave for your employees?
Cohort 2 · 5 questions
  1. q0Elevator pitch: tell us about your business, what brought you here, how you found this place, and one fact about yourself that everybody will remember.
  2. q1You are working from acceptance, not towards acceptance. What typically motivates you to work hard and create more? In what ways can you accept your identity in Christ and change your motivations to serve others? Or a story of showing love to a competitor or difficult customer.
  3. q1bAnybody have experience of praying for your customers, employees, or somebody who is disputing something with you?
  4. q2In your business or work, what does it look like for your motivation to be love itself — drafting contracts and offers through love? Have you practised that, and how does it feel?
  5. q3If we misplace our identity, what does it look like? Have you ever felt the target was silently misplaced and you were focused on something other than love? (Also put to the visiting pastor: misplaced identity in pastoral life.)
Cohort 3 · 5 questions
  1. q1Flow is built on one conviction: treat your customer as your neighbour. If you truly saw your next customer as a neighbour, which policy would you change first?
  2. q2At Flow, every price and detail is open to the customer. Where does withholding information give you an edge — and what would full honesty cost you?
  3. q3When a repair runs late or wrong, Flow eats the cost rather than passing it on. Where is God asking you to bear a cost so those you serve don't have to? (audio: 'Who's adopting the cost?')
  4. q4Don Flow worked every job in the dealership before asking anyone to trust him. Whose trust are you trying to earn, and what humble task might earn it?
  5. q5Flow's care for people isn't left to good intentions — it's built into pricing, warranties, and process. What value could you turn into a policy this quarter?

Where the numbers come from

Source. The Faith Driven Entrepreneur Foundations Singapore corpus: 24 sessions recorded between August 2024 and August 2026 across three cohorts, 2,349 minutes of video, 873 coded conversational turns of which 618 are participant speech from 289 distinct people. Every session was recorded and published as part of the chapter's ordinary practice.

How to read the figures. Depth is the coded strength of a contribution on a 1–3 scale. Theme percentages are the share of participant contributions in that session carrying the subject, against the share across all 618 participant contributions. Facilitator share is the facilitator's proportion of turns. Subgroup contrasts use Fisher's exact test with Benjamini–Hochberg correction within families; session-level associations use Spearman rank correlation over 24 points.

Limits. All coding was done by one coder, who also convened the sessions and wrote the questions, so every proportion is one rater's judgement and the thematic distribution reflects what was asked as well as what the room believes. Session-level parameters rest on 24 points, so the negative findings are failures to detect rather than demonstrations that no effect exists. Gender is unresolved for 100 of 420 appearance records. This is one chapter in one city and is best understood as an information-rich case rather than a sample.
