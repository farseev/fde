What each evening was like

Cohort 1. Met in a startup hub for 131 minutes with 23 voices in the room. The standard film was shown, taking 19 per cent of the evening. 5 questions were put. Coded depth rose from 1.64 in the introduction round to 2.5 in the discussion. The facilitator took 32 per cent of the turns.

The plan · about 104 minutes

Run of show and what to show

Plan for 9 to 23 people. The sequence is the one feature of the evening with a measurable effect, and it should be run whole.

Run of show

Timing

Ask what ordinary part of the business is assumed too small or too secular to matter. Close with a commitment round.

Which film

What to show

Standard

FDE promo, Kaestner's closing intro, the NatureSweet Tomatoes profile built on Isaiah 58.

Substitute used

Cohort 3 substituted the Sseko Designs profile with Liz Forkin Bohannon on sandals made in Uganda funding women's education.

Sseko is the stronger closer for a Singapore room, because it is a small company doing one clear thing.

A substitute was used in 1 of the three runs. Across all 24 sessions, substitution made no measurable difference to depth (2.10 against 2.19, p = 0.24). What it changes is the subject matter.

03
What was asked

Every question put, by cohort

Choose three. No recorded session got through more than about three at any one table, so the rest are a reserve rather than a plan.

Cohort 1 · 5 questions
  1. q0Everybody gonna introduce yourself just a little bit… tell us what brought you here and say some fun fact about yourself. That's mandatory.
  2. q1The great commission is found [in] Matthew [28]:19–20 where Jesus commands the disciple[s] to go and make other disciples… as a follower of Jesus, how can my faith-driven entrepreneurial exploit[s] serve the great commission? What are some ways you've been able to fulfill the great commission… through your business or whatever other endeavors you have?
  3. q2What kind of places in the world God brings you to, or what business [is] taking you [to]… and how [have] you seen your business become a place where people can see the love of Jesus even without realizing it?
  4. q3How do you make disci[ples] in the industry that you're working in?… are there any examples… that you spread it beyond your company — you start getting others to start an FDE, or go back to church again, or start making decisions differently in their business?
  5. q4What could it look like for you to take what you've learned from this series, particularly from the last se[ssion], to implement it this [week]… What are some first steps, initial decisions you could make and apply what you've learned?
Cohort 2 · 5 questions
  1. q0Introductions: your business, what brought you here, a fun fact about yourself, and how you make decisions in your business.
  2. q1From the tomato company video: what places has your business taken you that you weren't expecting? How have you seen your business become a place where people can see the love of Jesus even without realizing it?
  3. q2In which ways does the book of Acts show how significant the average business owner is in spreading the gospel? Stories of business people or employees doing things differently because of your example?
  4. q3What could it look like for you to be a disciple who makes disciples in your current industry?
  5. q4How could the story of Tyndale help someone who feels like they aren't impacting the world due to their secular job? In what ways does their story show us that working for a church isn't the only way to be part of the Great Commission?
Cohort 3 · 6 questions
  1. introWho you are, your business, what you do (elevator pitch), and a fun fact
  2. q1Dignity: [Sseko did not] just pay them; they help them see themselves as [image-]bearers. Right now in your business or work, where could you deliver the value without putting down [people's] dignity?
  3. q2Even a sandal company can be God's company: what ordinary part of your business do you assume is too small or too secular, where there is an opportunity to glorify God?
  4. q3A bridge, not a handout: rather than another donation or scholarship, is there a job (even a 'fictional role') you could create that could really amplify this?
  5. q4Sseko's Fellows distribution model (MLM-like, but people not product): what could your business or work tweak slightly to go global or scale, in a non-exploitative manner?
  6. q5Which story are you part of? Whose story in your organisation could you learn to see more fully?

Where the numbers come from

Source. The Faith Driven Entrepreneur Foundations Singapore corpus: 24 sessions recorded between August 2024 and August 2026 across three cohorts, 2,349 minutes of video, 873 coded conversational turns of which 618 are participant speech from 289 distinct people. Every session was recorded and published as part of the chapter's ordinary practice.

How to read the figures. Depth is the coded strength of a contribution on a 1–3 scale. Theme percentages are the share of participant contributions in that session carrying the subject, against the share across all 618 participant contributions. Facilitator share is the facilitator's proportion of turns. Subgroup contrasts use Fisher's exact test with Benjamini–Hochberg correction within families; session-level associations use Spearman rank correlation over 24 points.

Limits. All coding was done by one coder, who also convened the sessions and wrote the questions, so every proportion is one rater's judgement and the thematic distribution reflects what was asked as well as what the room believes. Session-level parameters rest on 24 points, so the negative findings are failures to detect rather than demonstrations that no effect exists. Gender is unresolved for 100 of 420 appearance records. This is one chapter in one city and is best understood as an information-rich case rather than a sample.

Citation. The analysis behind this guide is reported in full in A. Farseev, Christian Entrepreneurs and the Scripts of the Market: A Pentecostal Reading of Business Fellowship Talk in Singapore (under review). Published at faithdriven.sg/guide/ and may be reproduced with attribution.
