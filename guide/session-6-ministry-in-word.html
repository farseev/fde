What each evening was like

Cohort 1. Met in a startup hub for 88 minutes with 9 voices in the room. The standard film was shown, taking 26 per cent of the evening. 5 questions were put. Coded depth rose from 1.57 in the introduction round to 2.83 in the discussion. The facilitator took 31 per cent of the turns.

The plan · about 108 minutes

Run of show and what to show

Plan for 9 to 23 people. The sequence is the one feature of the evening with a measurable effect, and it should be run whole.

Which film

What to show

Standard

FDE promo, Kaestner on leading with a limp, the John and Ashley Marsh profile from Opelika, Alabama.

Substitute used

Cohort 3 substituted 'Not Called to Comfort' with Dave Hataj of Edgerton Gear.

The Hataj film frames the shop itself as the ministry, which is the question the room most wants to answer.

A substitute was used in 1 of the three runs. Across all 24 sessions, substitution made no measurable difference to depth (2.10 against 2.19, p = 0.24). What it changes is the subject matter.

03
What was asked

Every question put, by cohort

Choose three. No recorded session got through more than about three at any one table, so the rest are a reserve rather than a plan.

Cohort 1 · 5 questions
  1. q0We introduce each other… do it in the startup way… any fun facts about yourself?
  2. q1Did you have that kind of moment actually when you get saved? Anyone want to share… the one you feel like and then something changed?
  3. q2How does your relationship with God and his work [in your] personal life have an effect on your work life?
  4. q3You can pick up any of the three — how [does] it look like for you to walk with Christ in your workplace?
  5. q4When [has] failure made you better in your work? How has fail[ing] certain ventures help[ed] you rely more on the Lord… business failure is one thing but there may be some other failures that helped you to get closer to God?
Cohort 2 · 4 questions
  1. q0Very quick introductions — an elevator pitch: how do we make decisions, what brought us here, what our venture is doing — but please focus on how we bring Christ to our business.
  2. q1The focus of the session is to be dependent on God because we are helpless without him. How have you seen God's word in your personal life affecting your work life (and vice versa)?
  3. q2Second on-screen question (not read aloud): participants treat it as about work, success and grace/identity.
  4. q3What has it looked like for you to walk with Christ in your workplace?
Cohort 3 · 6 questions
  1. q1Dave returned to the one place he swore he'd never go back to. Where is God asking you to stay in something hard rather than escape to comfort?
  2. q2He expected ministry to mean leaving the shop; God showed him the shop was the ministry. How would your week change if your business itself were the pulpit God gave you?
  3. q3Labelled 'not college material,' Dave led from inadequacy and a broken culture. When you feel least qualified, what does leading anyway look like? (Alex reframed: 'in Asia, we actually cannot fail... cannot be weak')
  4. q4Through 'Craftsman with Character,' Dave gave overlooked kids a place to be seen and told the truth. Who is within earshot of your work, and what word can you speak?
  5. q5Dave's metric became people changed, not money — 'the company as congregation.' If you measured this quarter by people grown, what changes on Monday? (Alex reframed around AI agents and bringing 'the Christian thing' into the company)
  6. q6What did you do in your courses, your work? Did you talk to somebody about Jesus? And if not, then why?

Where the numbers come from

Source. The Faith Driven Entrepreneur Foundations Singapore corpus: 24 sessions recorded between August 2024 and August 2026 across three cohorts, 2,349 minutes of video, 873 coded conversational turns of which 618 are participant speech from 289 distinct people. Every session was recorded and published as part of the chapter's ordinary practice.

How to read the figures. Depth is the coded strength of a contribution on a 1–3 scale. Theme percentages are the share of participant contributions in that session carrying the subject, against the share across all 618 participant contributions. Facilitator share is the facilitator's proportion of turns. Subgroup contrasts use Fisher's exact test with Benjamini–Hochberg correction within families; session-level associations use Spearman rank correlation over 24 points.

Limits. All coding was done by one coder, who also convened the sessions and wrote the questions, so every proportion is one rater's judgement and the thematic distribution reflects what was asked as well as what the room believes. Session-level parameters rest on 24 points, so the negative findings are failures to detect rather than demonstrations that no effect exists. Gender is unresolved for 100 of 420 appearance records. This is one chapter in one city and is best understood as an information-rich case rather than a sample.

Citation. The analysis behind this guide is reported in full in A. Farseev, Christian Entrepreneurs and the Scripts of the Market: A Pentecostal Reading of Business Fellowship Talk in Singapore (under review). Published at faithdriven.sg/guide/ and may be reproduced with attribution.
