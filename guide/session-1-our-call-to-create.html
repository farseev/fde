What each evening was like

01
The plan · about 111 minutes

Run of show and what to show

Plan for 19 to 21 people. The sequence is the one feature of the evening with a measurable effect, and it should be run whole.

Run of show

Timing

  1. 0:00Arrive and welcomeSay why the room exists. Keep it short.10 min
  2. 0:10Introduction roundName, business, what brought them, a fun fact. Visible timer, one minute each.25 min
  3. 0:35FilmObserved film share for this session ran 15 to 23 per cent of the evening.15 min
  4. 0:50DiscussionThree questions keyed to the film, tables of six to eight, one recorder each.40 min
  5. 1:30Commitment and closeOne action each before next month, then pray.10 min

Ask for the faith-journey story explicitly in the opening round. This is the only session where people volunteer it.

Which film

What to show

Standard

Standard package: FDE promo reel, Justin and Henry Kaestner intros, the Dave Munson / Saddleback Leather profile, then J.D. Greear on the call to create.

Substitute used

Cohort 2 substituted two films, including the Pete Ochs prison-labour profile and the Praxis redemptive frame, and produced the deepest discussion in the corpus.

Either works. The substituted night scored highest, but that is one evening, and film substitution across all 24 sessions made no measurable difference to depth.

A substitute was used in 1 of the three runs. Across all 24 sessions, substitution made no measurable difference to depth (2.10 against 2.19, p = 0.24). What it changes is the subject matter.

What was asked

Every question put, by cohort

Choose three. No recorded session got through more than about three at any one table, so the rest are a reserve rather than a plan.

Cohort 1 · 7 questions
  1. q0Please introduce yourself: tell us about your life path and your path to God; your business and what brought you here; any fun fact about yourself.
  2. q1J.D. pinpointed the mindset that to be a Christian with an enterprise you must use awkward names and forced Christian language. What are some ways you've seen this approach displayed in your businesses, and what is necessary or unnecessary for having your faith displayed? (Alex added: did you share about Jesus with your employees or clients?)
  3. q2Bezalel and Oholiab as examples of excelling in God-given skills: in what ways could your work impact the world outside the church building? (Audio: 'how do you impact lives outside the church building?')
  4. q3Our role in the kingdom involves more than attending church services and paying tithes: in what ways can our work as Christian creators provide a purposeful and positive influence on our communities and local businesses? Why is it necessary for Christians to fulfil the call to create?
  5. q4You're a co-creator with God and a cultural change agent: in what ways could your work be a unique gift to the marketplace? (Audio, to Sarah: do you have culture shock, and how will you use your perspective and skills to empower Singapore?)
  6. q5Good versus perfect: in what ways do you see your business as an instrument God might use to redeem something that's broken or needs to be improved? Who are other entrepreneurs you've seen God use this way?
  7. q6Entrepreneurship should be worship — 'no more thinking you're fulfilling your role by going to church and putting money in the offering plate.' How do you relate to this; what does doing business as worship mean to you beyond going to church?

Where the numbers come from

Source. The Faith Driven Entrepreneur Foundations Singapore corpus: 24 sessions recorded between August 2024 and August 2026 across three cohorts, 2,349 minutes of video, 873 coded conversational turns of which 618 are participant speech from 289 distinct people. Every session was recorded and published as part of the chapter's ordinary practice.

How to read the figures. Depth is the coded strength of a contribution on a 1–3 scale. Theme percentages are the share of participant contributions in that session carrying the subject, against the share across all 618 participant contributions. Facilitator share is the facilitator's proportion of turns. Subgroup contrasts use Fisher's exact test with Benjamini–Hochberg correction within families; session-level associations use Spearman rank correlation over 24 points.

Limits. All coding was done by one coder, who also convened the sessions and wrote the questions, so every proportion is one rater's judgement and the thematic distribution reflects what was asked as well as what the room believes. Session-level parameters rest on 24 points, so the negative findings are failures to detect rather than demonstrations that no effect exists. Gender is unresolved for 100 of 420 appearance records. This is one chapter in one city and is best understood as an information-rich case rather than a sample.

Citation. The analysis behind this guide is reported in full in A. Farseev, Christian Entrepreneurs and the Scripts of the Market: A Pentecostal Reading of Business Fellowship Talk in Singapore (under review). Published at faithdriven.sg/guide/ and may be reproduced with attribution.
