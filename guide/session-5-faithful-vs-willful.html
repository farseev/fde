What was asked

Every question put, by cohort

Choose three. No recorded session got through more than about three at any one table, so the rest are a reserve rather than a plan.

Cohort 1 · 7 questions
  1. q0Introduce ourselves to each other — do like [an] elevator pitch and a fun fact.
  2. q1How do you measure your success? … What do you think about [identity] and what you say yours is?
  3. q2Who is the one who actually [does the] work — is this success, whose credit is this, God's or mine? … being spiritually mature versus being successful… how do you battle the temptation of doing that one last email, that one last thing every day?
  4. q3Given the video that we watched today, what you are motivated by, and do you have temptation to be motivated by something else, especially in Singapore?
  5. q4The roles [God has] called you [to] — how do you prioritize? … How have you dealt with balanc[ing] spiritual life, home life and work?
  6. q5What kind of habits do you practice in order to balance your work, home and spiritual life?
  7. q6Let's do a sprint, everybody going to share: for this coming week, how you going to rest… how do you incorporate more rest into your normal routine?
Cohort 2 · 5 questions
  1. q0Please introduce yourself and your company (1 min 'elevator' pitch): tell us about your business and what brought you here; any fun fact about yourself?
  2. q1What do you think about identity, and what do you say your identity is — an entrepreneur, a leader who just closed a funding round, an innovator — or to be loved, a son of God following his will? Did you face this kind of decision, and how was it made?
  3. q2Your business and career choices: times when you really wanted to do something but followed God's word — what was the result, and has it changed how you make decisions now?
  4. q3What aspects of the entrepreneurial journey have been most difficult for your family and other responsibilities? How do you balance them?
  5. q4'I sacrifice, we win': when you did something irrational in worldly terms in your business because you believed it was right / what God told you — have you had that kind of experience, and how did it end up?
Cohort 3 · 6 questions
  1. introPlease introduce yourself and your company (1 min “elevator” pitch). Tell us about your business and what brought you here! Any fun fact about yourself?
  2. q1Identity in Extremes: Lara Casey had to decouple her identity from a highly successful, glamorous 10-year-old media empire. Shady Hebeish had to decouple his identity from a catastrophic $20 million debt he inherited. How do both of these extremes—massive success and massive failure - threaten to become idols, and how can we anchor our identity in Christ regardless of what our balance sheet looks like?
  3. q2Defying Worldly Wisdom: Conventional business wisdom would have told Lara to keep her highly profitable magazine, and it would have told Shady to immediately declare bankruptcy on his father's mess. Yet, both followed counter-intuitive promptings from the Holy Spirit. Can you share a time when you felt God leading you to make a business decision that made zero sense on a spreadsheet?
  4. q3The Duality of Patience (Pruning vs. Enduring): Shady's faithfulness required a grueling, 20-year marathon of patiently paying off debt. Lara's faithfulness required the painful, immediate pruning of a healthy brand she spent a decade building. Where in your current business are you being called to patiently endure a long-term struggle, and where are you being called to abruptly "prune" a good thing to make room for God's best?
  5. q4The Necessity of Counsel: Lara relied heavily on the feedback and wise counsel of her C12 peer advisory group to make her bittersweet decision to retire Southern Weddings. Shady needed the alignment of his family and community to endure two decades of corporate turnaround. Why is it so dangerous to make major operational transitions in isolation, and who currently has the permission to speak hard truths into your leadership? ([FACILITATOR]'s reformulation [086:44]: 'last time you needed to make a decision, whether there was any community or communion with God or both that actually helped you')
  6. q5Redefining the "Win": Shady found himself at the center of a redemptive story that started in a deep hole, while Lara realized that true flourishing meant shutting down her biggest brand. Both stories destroy the assumption that obedience to God automatically equals instant financial growth. If God's ultimate metric for your business isn't financial scale or market dominance, how does that change the way you measure your success this upcoming quarter?

Where the numbers come from

Source. The Faith Driven Entrepreneur Foundations Singapore corpus: 24 sessions recorded between August 2024 and August 2026 across three cohorts, 2,349 minutes of video, 873 coded conversational turns of which 618 are participant speech from 289 distinct people. Every session was recorded and published as part of the chapter's ordinary practice.

How to read the figures. Depth is the number of doctrinal markers carried by a contribution, averaged over the contributions in a segment. Theme percentages are the share of participant contributions in that session carrying the subject, against the share across all 618 participant contributions. Facilitator share is the facilitator's proportion of turns. Subgroup contrasts use Fisher's exact test with Benjamini–Hochberg correction within families; session-level associations use Spearman rank correlation over 24 points.

Reliability. Three large language models independently recoded a stratified sample of seventy-two contributions from the codebook alone. Cohen's κ against the original coding runs 0.67, 0.63 and 0.57 averaged over the four doctrinal families, and 0.73 to 0.84 on the Pentecostal family that carries the gender finding. The models mark more sparingly than the original coding, so the proportions here are if anything conservative. The stance scheme is unattested.

Limits. The marker scheme rests on a single coding pass, and the questions were written by the convenor, so the thematic distribution reflects what was asked as well as what the room believes. Session-level parameters rest on 24 points, so the negative findings are failures to detect rather than demonstrations that no effect exists. Gender is unresolved for 100 of 420 appearance records. This is one chapter in one city and is best understood as an information-rich case rather than a sample.
