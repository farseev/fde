The coded corpus, open.

Every coded turn behind this guide and the accompanying article, anonymised and released for reuse. Speakers carry pseudonymous codes with sex attached, so a reader can follow the same person across sessions and check any claim without learning who they are.

Anonymisation

What was removed, and what remains

Speaker codes

Traceable without being named

Each participant has a code carrying their sex: F for women, M for men, U where sex could not be resolved from the recording. The code is stable, so F012 in session two and F012 in session six are the same person. A trailing x marks a speaker who could not be linked across sessions, usually because their name was never audible. The convenor's turns are marked FAC and are excluded from every statistic in the article.

Redaction

Conservative, and not a guarantee

Participant names inside the transcribed speech are replaced by the speaker's code, and the convenor's name by [FACILITATOR]. Redaction is deliberately conservative: several participants are named Faith, Grace, Joy and Mark, and scrubbing those words would have destroyed the theological content the corpus exists to measure. Those first names therefore remain where they occur as ordinary words. Company names, church names and places are retained because the analysis depends on them. Anyone who attended these sessions may still recognise a contribution.

The fourth marker family

Why it is called ascetic-kenotic and not Orthodox

The A codes were first specified as Eastern Orthodox, anchored to the Philokalia, Ware and Lossky. An audit of the coded quotations found that 88 per cent of the family's instances fall in three codes whose content is pan-Christian: disclosure of suffering, detachment from worldly measures of success, and gradual transformation. No quotation invokes a practice distinctive to Orthodoxy, and the speakers attend Pentecostal, evangelical, Lutheran, Presbyterian and Catholic congregations. The family is therefore published as a cross-traditional register rather than a confessional tradition, and is excluded from tests of ecclesial affiliation. Code numbering is unchanged: A1 was O1, and so on.

Access

The archive is password-protected

The dataset ships as a ZIP containing a single CSV. The archive carries a password, so the file cannot be opened straight from a crawler or an automated scrape. Researchers can obtain the password from a@faithdriven.sg, stating briefly what the data will be used for. The contents are already anonymised; the password restricts casual access, not determined access.

Where the numbers come from

Source. The Faith Driven Entrepreneur Foundations Singapore corpus: 24 sessions recorded between August 2024 and August 2026 across three cohorts, 2,349 minutes of video, 873 coded conversational turns of which 618 are participant speech from 289 distinct people. Every session was recorded and published as part of the chapter's ordinary practice.

How to read the figures. Depth is the coded strength of a contribution on a 1–3 scale. Theme percentages are the share of participant contributions in that session carrying the subject, against the share across all 618 participant contributions. Facilitator share is the facilitator's proportion of turns. Subgroup contrasts use Fisher's exact test with Benjamini–Hochberg correction within families; session-level associations use Spearman rank correlation over 24 points.

Limits. All coding was done by one coder, who also convened the sessions and wrote the questions, so every proportion is one rater's judgement and the thematic distribution reflects what was asked as well as what the room believes. Session-level parameters rest on 24 points, so the negative findings are failures to detect rather than demonstrations that no effect exists. Gender is unresolved for 100 of 420 appearance records. This is one chapter in one city and is best understood as an information-rich case rather than a sample.

Citation. The analysis behind this guide is reported in full in A. Farseev, Christian Entrepreneurs and the Scripts of the Market: A Pentecostal Reading of Business Fellowship Talk in Singapore (under review). Published at faithdriven.sg/guide/ and may be reproduced with attribution.
