Eight sessions, measured. Run them on evidence.

Every Foundations session our Singapore chapter has run since August 2024 was recorded, transcribed and coded. This guide reports what each of the eight sessions actually produces in the room, which questions were put, who speaks, and which of the things convenors worry about turn out not to matter. Every recommendation carries the measurement behind it.

Before the eight

The shape of a session

The sequence is the one feature of a Foundations evening with a measurable effect, and it should be run whole. The chapter's own practice sets the scale: a mean of 98 minutes, from 75 to 131, with 8 to 27 voices in the room and four to eight questions put, a median of six.

Run of show

About 100 minutes

  1. 0:00Arrive and welcomeSay why the room exists. Keep it short.10 min
  2. 0:10Introduction roundEveryone speaks: name, business, what brought them, a fun fact.25 min
  3. 0:35FilmThe teaching segment. Median across recorded sessions is 23 per cent of the evening.15 min
  4. 0:50DiscussionThree questions keyed to the film, tables of six to eight.40 min
  5. 1:30Commitment and closeOne action each before next month, then pray.10 min
Why the order matters

The two segments do different work

They are not interchangeable. Introduction rounds are where calling and the church come up; discussion is where ethics, identity, risk and suffering rise. Across the corpus, calling appears in 49 per cent of introduction turns and falls to 23 in discussion, while ethics and excellence rise from 4 per cent to 34, Spirit-guidance from 10 to 34, identity from 8 to 29, and both risk and suffering from 6 to 21.

An introduction round cannot do the work of a discussion, and a discussion without one starts cold. In all 24 recorded sessions the discussion ran deeper than the round preceding it.

The comparison is between segments of a fixed format rather than between conditions, so it shows the sequence doing what it is designed to do. It does not show that an alternative format would do less.

What the evidence says

Six findings that should change how you run it

Each carries the measurement behind it. Where a result is suggestive rather than established, it says so.

24 of 24 sessions

Run the sequence whole

The introduction round followed by a film followed by keyed questions is the one feature with a measurable effect. In every one of the 24 recorded sessions the discussion ran deeper than the introduction round before it, mean coded depth rising from 1.67 to 2.52 on a 1-3 scale (paired Wilcoxon p = 1.2 x 10^-7).

DoNever drop the introduction round to save time.

rho = -0.55, q = 0.020

Split rooms above sixteen

More voices meant fewer turns each, with no gain in depth. Recorded rooms ran from 8 to 27 voices.

DoTables of six to eight, each with a briefed host.

14% to 60%

Talk less than you think

Facilitator share of turns varied fourfold across recorded sessions. Higher share went with fewer participant turns (rho = -0.46, p = 0.027) and no more depth (rho = +0.01). The correlation does not survive multiple-comparison correction.

DoAim under a third of the turns, and treat this as a direction rather than a proven effect.

p = 0.24

Do not agonise over the film

Across 15 standard and 9 substituted sessions, coded depth was indistinguishable. What a substitute film changes is the subject matter, not the quality of the conversation.

DoPick the film for the conversation you want, then write questions about the film you actually showed.

224 arrangements

Meet wherever is convenient

Three measures appeared to separate sessions held in worship buildings from those held elsewhere, at q = 0.069. All three collapse under an exact permutation test that holds cohort fixed.

DoThe room does not change what gets said. Choose for parking and quiet.

q = 0.013 to 0.046

Expect the room to divide by sex

Women's contributions carry Pentecostal-charismatic markers at 30.4 per cent of their weighted marker mass against 21.1 for men, and raise Spirit-guidance and prayer at 30.1 against 18.3. Men raise wealth and stewardship at 24.3 against 13.8. This is the most robust contrast in the corpus.

DoWhen the conversation turns to money, invite the women in early, and the reverse when it turns to guidance.

Answers

Common questions

How long should a Faith Driven Entrepreneur Foundations session run?

Across 24 recorded sessions the mean length was 98 minutes, ranging from 75 to 131. Longer sessions produced more turns of conversation (Spearman rho = 0.64, p = 0.0007) but not deeper ones, so roughly 90 to 110 minutes is the working range.

Does the opening introduction round matter?

Yes. In 24 of 24 recorded sessions the discussion that followed was deeper than the introduction round preceding it, with mean coded depth rising from 1.67 to 2.52 on a 1–3 scale. The two segments also elicit different subjects: calling and the church dominate introductions, while ethics, identity, risk and suffering rise in discussion.

How many people should be in the room?

More voices meant fewer turns each (rho = -0.55, q = 0.020) with no gain in depth. Recorded rooms ran from 8 to 27 voices. Above roughly sixteen, split into tables of six to eight.

Does it matter which film is shown?

Less than convenors assume. Across 15 sessions using the standard film and 9 using a substitute, coded depth was statistically indistinguishable (2.10 against 2.19, p = 0.24). What the film changes is the subject matter, not the depth.

Does the venue change the conversation?

No effect survives testing. Three measures appeared to separate sessions held in worship buildings from those held elsewhere, but all three collapse once cohort is held fixed by an exact permutation test. Meet wherever is convenient.

How much should the facilitator talk?

Less. Facilitator share ranged from 14 to 60 per cent of turns. Higher facilitator share was associated with fewer participant turns (rho = -0.46, p = 0.027), though this does not survive multiple-comparison correction. Treat it as a direction.

Do men and women contribute differently?

Yes, and it is the most robust contrast in the corpus. Women's turns carry Pentecostal-charismatic markers at 30.4 per cent of their weighted marker mass against 21.1 per cent for men (q = 0.013), and raise Spirit-guidance and prayer at 30.1 per cent against 18.3 (q = 0.036). Men raise wealth and stewardship at 24.3 per cent against 13.8 (q = 0.046).

How many questions should be asked in a session?

Between four and eight were put in recorded sessions, with a median of six. No recorded session got through more than about three at any one table. Choose three and drop the rest.

Where the numbers come from

Source. The Faith Driven Entrepreneur Foundations Singapore corpus: 24 sessions recorded between August 2024 and August 2026 across three cohorts, 2,349 minutes of video, 873 coded conversational turns of which 618 are participant speech from 289 distinct people. Every session was recorded and published as part of the chapter's ordinary practice.

How to read the figures. Depth is the coded strength of a contribution on a 1–3 scale. Theme percentages are the share of participant contributions in that session carrying the subject, against the share across all 618 participant contributions. Facilitator share is the facilitator's proportion of turns. Subgroup contrasts use Fisher's exact test with Benjamini–Hochberg correction within families; session-level associations use Spearman rank correlation over 24 points.

Limits. All coding was done by one coder, who also convened the sessions and wrote the questions, so every proportion is one rater's judgement and the thematic distribution reflects what was asked as well as what the room believes. Session-level parameters rest on 24 points, so the negative findings are failures to detect rather than demonstrations that no effect exists. Gender is unresolved for 100 of 420 appearance records. This is one chapter in one city and is best understood as an information-rich case rather than a sample.

Citation. The analysis behind this guide is reported in full in A. Farseev, Christian Entrepreneurs and the Scripts of the Market: A Pentecostal Reading of Business Fellowship Talk in Singapore (under review). Published at faithdriven.sg/guide/ and may be reproduced with attribution.
