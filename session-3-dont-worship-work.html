  1. q0Please introduce yourself and your company (1-minute elevator pitch): tell us about your business, what brought you here, and a fun fact.
  2. q1J.D. gave grim statistics about entrepreneurs (depression, ADHD, substance abuse, suicidal thoughts). What do you believe are the reasons behind those statistics, and in what ways have you dealt with them? (Audio: [FACILITATOR] asks for one entrepreneur and one VC/family-office perspective.)

  5. q4Faulty justice systems can make life feel meaningless. How have you seen good actions go unrewarded in your entrepreneurship journey? In what ways can keeping your eyes on Jesus help with the stress of this principle?
Cohort 2 · 6 questions
  1. q0Please introduce yourself and your company (1 min 'elevator' pitch): tell us about your business and what brought you here; any fun fact about yourself?
  2. q1Since success isn't guaranteed in this life, all we can do is take wise risks and try to live a balanced life. What could taking a healthy or wise risk look like for you?
  3. q2J.D. gave grim statistics about entrepreneurs (depression, ADHD, substance abuse). What do you believe are some of the reasons behind those statistics? In what ways have you dealt with any of these issues?
  4. q3Did you experience that you were quite unclear why certain things were happening, and then later realised that was the reason behind it?
  5. q4How have you been disappointed by a certain pleasure (a milestone reached that felt empty)? What are some ways that Christians in business can strive for success without pleasure being the ultimate goal?
  6. q5As a Christian, how do you typically respond when you feel cheated out of an opportunity for success? What could it look like for Christians in business to respond graciously when things don't work in their favor?
Cohort 3 · 7 questions
  1. introPlease introduce yourself and your company (1 min “elevator” pitch). Tell us about your business and what brought you here! Any fun fact about yourself?
  2. q1The Idol of Work: Peter Greer warns that entrepreneurs often turn God's call to create into a "permission slip to be a workaholic." How do you personally distinguish between a healthy, God-given drive to build and an unhealthy worship of your work? What is one way ambition subtly steals your attention from the things that matter most?
  3. q2The Pressure to Compromise: During WWII, Correct Craft faced intense government pressure to work seven days a week to build 400 boats in 30 days. They refused to work on Sundays, trusting God for a "miracle." When have you felt intense pressure to compromise a core conviction just to survive or scale your business? How easy is it for you to actually stop and rest when the math says you shouldn't? (Audio framing: 'how would you still choose to do the Sabbath, to go to church, to join the ministry?')
  4. q3The Cost of Culture: Bill Yeargin noted that operating out of a strong faith culture sometimes means making decisions that negatively impact the bottom line. Has your faith ever cost you a financial opportunity, a partnership, or a competitive edge? How do you handle the tension when honoring God contradicts standard "business sense"?
  5. q4The Workplace as Ministry: Correct Craft views the workplace as a venue to reach people who might never step inside a church. How does your company currently serve as a ministry to your employees, vendors, or customers? What is one practical way you can use your business to care for the people under your leadership?
  6. q5Sabbath as Surrender: Peter Greer described resting on the Sabbath as our way of saying "amen" to God's design. How consistent is your practice of Sabbath? What specific fears or anxieties surface when you try to completely disconnect from your business for a full day? (Not asked separately in audio.)
  7. q6Your Ultimate Mission: Correct Craft's mission is "Building boats to the glory of God." If you had to summarize the eternal mission of your business in a single sentence, what would it be? What is one adjustment you need to make to your schedule this week to reflect that higher mission? (Audio: 'Anybody come up with a redemptive vision for your business?')

Where the numbers come from

Source. The Faith Driven Entrepreneur Foundations Singapore corpus: 24 sessions recorded between August 2024 and August 2026 across three cohorts, 2,349 minutes of video, 873 coded conversational turns of which 618 are participant speech from 289 distinct people. Every session was recorded and published as part of the chapter's ordinary practice.

How to read the figures. Depth is the number of doctrinal markers carried by a contribution, averaged over the contributions in a segment. Theme percentages are the share of participant contributions in that session carrying the subject, against the share across all 618 participant contributions. Facilitator share is the facilitator's proportion of turns. Subgroup contrasts use Fisher's exact test with Benjamini–Hochberg correction within families; session-level associations use Spearman rank correlation over 24 points.

Reliability. Three large language models independently recoded a stratified sample of seventy-two contributions from the codebook alone. Cohen's κ against the original coding runs 0.67, 0.63 and 0.57 averaged over the four doctrinal families, and 0.73 to 0.84 on the Pentecostal family that carries the gender finding. The models mark more sparingly than the original coding, so the proportions here are if anything conservative. The stance scheme is unattested.

Limits. The marker scheme rests on a single coding pass, and the questions were written by the convenor, so the thematic distribution reflects what was asked as well as what the room believes. Session-level parameters rest on 24 points, so the negative findings are failures to detect rather than demonstrations that no effect exists. Gender is unresolved for 100 of 420 appearance records. This is one chapter in one city and is best understood as an information-rich case rather than a sample.
