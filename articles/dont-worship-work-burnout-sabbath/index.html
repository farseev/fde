Faith at work

Don't worship work: burnout, hustle and the Sabbath

What FDE's third session teaches, what Singapore founders said when they answered honestly, and a practical way to start working from rest.

Quick answer

"Don't Worship Work" is Session 3 of the Faith Driven Entrepreneur Foundation Course. Its argument is that work makes a good servant but a cruel god. When a business becomes the source of your identity, its failures become your failures and its demands never end. In Singapore's sessions, founders linked burnout and depression directly to unicorn expectations and to family scripts about success. The antidote they practised was Sabbath, understood "not to rest from work, but rather to start working from that rest."

Why do founders burn out?

Pressure is part of building, but three forces make it worse for many Asian founders:

  1. Venture math. "It's always 'be a unicorn, be a unicorn'. VCs coming to you: you better 100x, or at least 10x… hustle, hustle. It led to that burnout," said a former founder who also described depression (watch).
  2. Family scripts. "The traditional Chinese family always thought money is probably the most important thing. Success has to be successful; there's no other alternative."
  3. Identity fusion. "My happiness used to be: when can I make my first $10 million… and be on the Forbes list."

A mentor of 17 years added a sobering point: "If you're absolutely completely normal you wouldn't be an entrepreneur… we're more vulnerable people."

What does "Don't Worship Work" teach?

The session's teaching walks through Ecclesiastes. Solomon had everything (wealth, projects, pleasure, wisdom) and found it all "meaningless" when pursued for its own sake. The standard film profiles Point B, an employee-owned US consultancy, and Henry Kaestner describes the values he built into Bandwidth. The point is not that work is bad. Work was made to serve something bigger than you, and it can't carry the weight of being your god.

What is the alternative? Identity that survives failure

This is what Phil Chen of the New Taipei Kings means by "your attention is your worship" (read his story). Whatever you can't stop thinking about is what you are actually serving.

How do Christian entrepreneurs practise Sabbath?

Several Singapore founders described concrete practices:

  • Working from rest. "The concept is not to rest from work, but rather to start working from that rest… the rest that we set aside is really to prepare ourselves." (watch)
  • A 24-hour Sabbath, for 12 or 13 years. "It's actually been the practice that's helped me accelerate… I know that at the end of the day, I have something to fall back on." (watch)
  • Weekly prayer walks. Friday morning walks in the park to slow down, with a reminder that "we spend so much on our houses, but we are the temple of the Holy Spirit".
  • Treating your body as a gift. "My own mental and physical health is my number one priority… I would notice very clearly if it's too much work or the wrong work."

The cost of keeping Sabbath

It isn't always easy. One employee described colleagues complaining about "one less pair of hands" on a religious rest day, and a case that went "all the way up to top management". The room didn't pretend Sabbath is free. It treated it as a form of faithfulness that sometimes costs you something.

A simple Sabbath plan for founders

  1. Pick a 24-hour window and put it in your calendar like a board meeting.
  2. Tell your team and set an auto-reply. Name a delegate for real emergencies.
  3. Remove the dashboards: log out of analytics, bank and ad accounts.
  4. Add something life-giving, such as worship, a long meal, a walk or play with your children.
  5. Start the week from rest: on Monday morning, pray before you open the laptop.
  6. Review after a month. Most founders find their decisions got better, not worse.

Frequently asked questions

What does "Don't Worship Work" mean?

It is the third session of the Faith Driven Entrepreneur Foundation Course. It teaches that work is good but becomes destructive when it turns into the source of your identity and worth, which is effectively worshipping it.

How can Christian founders avoid burnout?

Singapore's faith-driven founders point to three practices: rooting identity in God rather than in business results, keeping a regular Sabbath, and belonging to a community of peers who understand the pressure.

Can a startup founder really take a full day off each week?

Founders in our sessions who have kept a weekly Sabbath for over a decade say it helped them achieve more, not less, because they work from rest instead of toward it.
