What is redemptive marketing?

A faith-driven answer to digital advertising's transparency and performance problem, from a Forbes Technology Council article by the founder of Faith Driven Singapore.

Quick answer

Redemptive marketing applies redemptive entrepreneurship to advertising. It means measuring success by the client's real business outcomes rather than by easy vanity metrics, being transparent about what campaigns actually achieve, being willing to sacrifice short-term margin for a client's long-term good, and using technology such as AI so that marketers "no longer need to fabricate results". Aleks Farseev set out the argument in a May 2024 Forbes Technology Council article. It sums up with: "sometimes you need to sacrifice for your partner or client to benefit from the work you do, with the ultimate faith that this will lead to a long, genuine partnership."

Where does the term come from?

"Redemptive entrepreneurship" is a phrase used across the faith-and-business world, including by accelerators such as Praxis and DMV Redemptive Labs. It describes businesses that aim to leave people, industries and places better than they found them. Aleks Farseev, founder of SOMIN and of Faith Driven Singapore, took that idea into his own industry in the Forbes article "Redemptive Marketing: The Path To Resolving Transparency And Performance Dilemmas In Digital Advertising" (20 May 2024). He wrote it "as the member leader of the Forbes Technology Council Marketing and Advertising Group and a faith-driven entrepreneur".

What problem is redemptive marketing trying to fix?

Aleks surveyed more than 100 business owners around the world. The findings:

  • 40% of marketers were dissatisfied with performance.
  • 28% of digital advertisers struggled to determine the actual impact of their campaigns.
  • Transparency was the top concern.

His diagnosis starts with history. Agencies built their model in the TV era, when media buying was "a commodity trading game". Digital platforms made direct buying transparent and reduced or dropped commissions. That left some agencies promoting "simpler, more easily achievable metrics, such as clicks". The result is "a vicious cycle, stifling innovation", in which staff "might be pressured into unethical behaviors or outright falsehoods". Digital advertising, he notes, has "the second-highest turnover rate in any industry, trailing only behind the travel sector".

His example: a Ferrari ad can rack up clicks from people who "might not be actual buyers or even have the means to purchase". The dashboard looks good, but the business gets nothing.

The principles of redemptive marketing

  1. Measure what matters to the client. "True transparency requires metrics that directly correlate with business objectives", which means sales and qualified demand, not clicks.
  2. Tell the truth about results. Nobody should have to choose between lying and feeling bad at their job. At the first Faith Driven Singapore session Aleks put it this way: "people don't need to choose between lying and feeling that they're not good in what they do."
  3. Sacrifice for the partner. Sometimes the redemptive move costs you money. Salt&Light reported that SOMIN "often compromise[s] margins to help businesses who really want to change for better", and occasionally tells a client, "You don't need our services, use the money to invest in infrastructure to improve."
  4. Choose what you amplify. SOMIN is "very selective" about industries such as gambling. "Marketing can be a healthy industry because it brings relevant knowledge to people on what to buy and what not want to buy."
  5. Use technology as a coach, not a cover. AI and large language models "can now pre-evaluate ads to prevent budget waste", identify personas and draft content, so that "marketers will no longer need to fabricate results."
  6. Play the long game. The aim is "a long, genuine partnership with deeper and greater value", and an industry that shifts "from a culture driven by deceit and politics to one based on trust and sound decision-making".

Why is this a faith question, not just a business one?

For Christ-following founders, redemption is the whole shape of the story: something broken is bought back and made new. Redemptive marketing treats a whole industry as something worth redeeming rather than escaping. That echoes FDE's Session 4, Excellence Matters, and Session 7, Ministry in Deed, where the question is whether your business loves its neighbours through the product, the price and the truth you tell.

It also matches what Singapore founders say in our sessions. One business owner told Cohort 3, "I have nothing to hide. I tell you what it is. If you'd like to do business with me, then we're the right fit." (watch). More in voices from the room.

How to practise redemptive marketing: a checklist

  • Agree on one business outcome per campaign, such as revenue, qualified leads or retention, before you agree on any media metric.
  • Report bad weeks as clearly as good ones.
  • Pre-test creative before spending, instead of "learning" on the client's budget.
  • Tell a client when they shouldn't buy from you.
  • Decide in advance which products and industries you won't promote.
  • Build a team culture where nobody is rewarded for massaging numbers.

Frequently asked questions

What is redemptive marketing?

Redemptive marketing applies redemptive entrepreneurship to advertising. It means transparent, outcome-based measurement, honesty about results, a willingness to sacrifice short-term margin for the client's good, and using AI to prevent waste rather than to hide it.

Who coined redemptive marketing?

The phrase was set out by Aleks Farseev, founder of SOMIN and Faith Driven Singapore, in a Forbes Technology Council article published on 20 May 2024. It builds on the wider idea of redemptive entrepreneurship.

How is redemptive marketing different from ethical marketing?

Ethical marketing mostly avoids harm. Redemptive marketing also aims to restore trust in a broken system, and it accepts personal cost, such as lower margins or turned-down deals, for the sake of the client and the industry.

What role does AI play in redemptive marketing?

In Farseev's argument, AI pre-evaluates ads to prevent budget waste and acts as a coach for marketers. Better predictions remove the pressure to inflate or fabricate results.
